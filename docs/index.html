The idea Skeleton coordinates keep motion, but can lose the cues that tell actions apart. PoseBridge preserves those cues inside the pose estimator and carries them into zero-shot recognition.

01 / THE PROBLEM

Similar motion. Different actions.

Putting on a hat, headphones, or glasses can produce similar hand-to-head trajectories. Joint coordinates alone do not make the object and its interaction with the body explicit.

↳

Bridge the information loss at its source. Use the pose estimator as a source of body-grounded semantics, alongside the skeletons it already produces.

Read the abstract +

Zero-shot skeleton-based action recognition (ZSSAR) is typically treated as a skeleton-text alignment problem: encode joint-coordinate sequences, align them with language, and classify unseen actions. We argue that this alignment is often too late. Skeletons are not complete action observations, but compressed outputs of human pose estimation (HPE); by the time alignment begins, human-object interactions and pose-relative visual cues may no longer be explicit. We call this upstream semantic loss.

To address it, we propose PoseBridge, an HPE-aware ZSSAR framework that bridges intermediate HPE representations to skeleton-text alignment. Rather than adding an RGB action branch or object detector, PoseBridge extracts pose-anchored semantic cues from the same HPE process that produces skeletons, then transfers them through skeleton-conditioned bridging and semantic prototype adaptation.

Across NTU-RGB+D 60/120, PKU-MMD, and Kinetics-200/400, PoseBridge improves ZSSAR performance under the evaluated protocols. On the Kinetics-200/400 PURLS benchmark, which contains in-the-wild videos with diverse scenes and action contexts, PoseBridge shows the clearest separation, improving the strongest compared baseline by 13.3–17.4 points across all eight splits. Our code will be publicly released.

02 / THE METHOD

Preserve first. Then bridge.

Two coupled stages carry pose-anchored semantics from human pose estimation into skeleton-text alignment.

STAGE 01 · WITHIN HPE

Keep the visual evidence
around the body.

Hierarchical refinement injects fine-grained shallow features into deeper pose-structured features. Body-aware pooling selects cues around predicted joints.

Caption-based semantic alignment encourages these cues to retain action-relevant meaning while preserving the pose estimation objective.

OUTPUT Skeletons + pose-anchored semantic cues
STAGE 02 · ZERO-SHOT RECOGNITION

Connect motion and meaning.

The frozen pose estimator supplies skeletons and cached semantic cues. A skeleton-conditioned bridge lets motion query the temporal cues; prototype adaptation brings language targets closer to the pose-semantic space.

The recognition setting

PoseBridge uses skeletons, intermediate HPE cues, and text. The same pose estimation pass provides both visual outputs; an independent RGB action backbone or object detector is not needed. Unseen-class prototypes use shifts learned from related seen classes, with no unseen visual samples.

04 / A CLOSER LOOK

Recover the cues that distinguish actions.

“Folding paper” and “typing on a keyboard” can look similar in skeleton space. Pose-anchored cues provide evidence around the hands and manipulated objects.

ATTENTIONFrom body localization to action-relevant regions. PoseBridge emphasizes hand-object interaction areas in these examples.

REPRESENTATIONClearer separation for motion-ambiguous actions. The illustrated zero-shot matching features are less mixed.

Scope and limitations +

PoseBridge assumes access to intermediate pose-estimation features during skeleton extraction, so it uses an HPE-aware protocol. Its cues can become unreliable under heavy occlusion, severe motion blur, pose estimation failures, or when objects are very small or outside the body-attended region. See Appendix G for the full discussion.
